1. Defining and Problematizing the Issue

Problem Definition: The Philippines faces energy vulnerability due to imported coal and oil dependency, leading to regional-high power bills. While nuclear provides low-fuel-cost baseload power, past failures like BNPP risk long-term debt without power generation if poorly implemented.
Root Causes: Challenges span economics, political divisions around BNPP Westinghouse corruption, and social fears following Chernobyl/Fukushima, alongside seismic risks along the Pacific Ring of Fire.

2. Actors & Synthesized Stance

01 / PROPONENTS

DOE, DOST, Technical Experts

Advocate for staged competitive procurement to deliver 1,200 MW baseload capacity by 2032 under PhilATOM oversight and EO 116.

02 / CRITICS

Greenpeace & Civil Advocates

Highlight radioactive waste risks, ring-of-fire natural hazards, and high upfront capital, advocating for 100% renewables.

03 / SYNTHESIS

Our Position (Group 2)

Support nuclear power conditionally: strictly bound by independent IAEA audits, PhilATOM regulation, and integration with geothermal, solar, and wind.

4. Strategic Roadmap (3-Stage Framework)

STAGE 1: PREPARE

Strengthen PhilATOM regulation under RA 12305, train workforce, establish waste management, and complete IAEA Milestone reviews before construction.

STAGE 2: PROTECT & FINANCE

Mobilize private capital via structured PPPs while enforcing multi-stakeholder and international audits to ensure safety supersedes profit.

STAGE 3: EXPAND RESPONSIBLY

Deliver 1,200 MW baseload power to the main grid and deploy Small Modular Reactors (SMRs) to off-grid islands replacing diesel barges.

Evaluating Overall Success Indicators

Feasibility & Execution

A well-designed framework cannot succeed if it is poorly implemented. We will evaluate whether the solution is executed as planned by ensuring all necessary resources, activities, and government-private stakeholders are fully in place. To guarantee the solution is affordable and efficient, we will continuously compare the actual expenses, time, and resources utilized by the Public-Private Partnerships against the available national budget constraints.

Budgetary & Resource Conformance

Return of Investment (ROI)

We will quantify success by measuring the percentage of profit and cost-savings generated in relation to the capital spent by PPPs within a reasonable timeframe after deployment, comparing actual expenses to the national energy budget.

Capital Efficiency & Savings

User Satisfaction

Success requires long-term public adaptation. We will utilize continuous community surveys and public consultations in affected provinces to gather feedback, ensuring that if opportunities for improvement arise, the solution can be refined.

Public Trust & Consultation

Sustainability & Comparative Performance

We will establish a step-by-step review routine to ensure long-term quality. We will compare our solution's carbon emission metrics and cost stability against previous coal-dependent grids to ensure the nuclear framework performs better than past systems.

Decarbonization & Stability

Future Recommendations & Challenges

A major challenge is managing proprietary business data in PPPs while ensuring public safety disclosures, as well as navigating maritime logistics for SMRs during typhoon seasons. Future implementation must prioritize maritime transport safety laws and transparent public-private data-sharing agreements to maintain trust.
